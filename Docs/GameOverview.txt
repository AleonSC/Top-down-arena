TOP-DOWN ARENA: THE GAME IN PLAIN WORDS
=======================================

This is a plain-language description of the whole game: what it is, how a run
plays out, and how each system fits in. It is meant for someone who has never
seen the game. For exact numbers, see README.md.


1. THE BIG PICTURE
------------------

Top-Down Arena is a co-op "bullet hell" game. You look down on a flat arena,
move around with the keyboard, aim with the mouse, and fight waves of enemies
that fire patterns of bullets at you. One to three players can play together
over the network. The game is built in Unity 6, and every picture in it is
drawn by code, so there are no art files.

The goal is to survive 50 waves. Every 5th wave is a boss fight, so there are
ten bosses. Beat the last one, The Eclipse, and you win. After a win, the host
can keep going in "endless" mode, where the bosses come around again and get
tougher.

The run ends in defeat if every player is down at the same time.


2. THE ARENA
------------

- The arena is a single screen, 1600 by 900. It wraps around for players: walk
  off the left edge and you come in on the right. Enemies and bullets do not
  wrap, so the edges are a good escape route.
- Your real hitbox is only the small coloured dot in the middle of your
  character, not the whole body. That is what makes weaving through bullets
  possible.
- The arena holds props: crates you can hide behind (enemy shots stop at them,
  yours fly over), pots that break into health or XP, and explosive barrels.
  Treasure chests turn up now and then. In boss fights, health barrels drop in.
- Several things can change the arena: paths, modifiers and boss mechanics (see
  below).


3. PICKING A CHARACTER
----------------------

Before a run, each player picks a role, a class, a weapon and a Space ability.

ROLE sets your job and your health:
- Tank: 240 health. Enemies prefer to target you. You soak damage.
- Healer: 140 health. You have a healing aura around you that mends allies.
- DPS: 95 health. You deal the most damage but can take the least.

CLASS sets your style. There are six:
- Soldier: guns and gear.
- Elementalist: fire, frost, lightning and arcane spells.
- Summoner: you bring minions (wolves, bats, golems, sprites) that fight for you
  and stay with you between waves.
- Knight: melee. Holding fire swings a weapon instead, and the swing can cut
  small bullets out of the air. Knights are armoured and hit hard.
- Ninja: fast, lightly protected, with thrown blades, quick melee and tricks
  like teleporting behind an enemy.
- Hemomancer: blood magic. Every Space ability costs you health, but you deal
  more damage the lower your health is, and your hits heal you a little.

Each role and class pairing gets its own three weapons and three Space
abilities to choose from, plus one fixed ultimate. Several players can pick
the same role if they like.

The game also has a lobby where you choose all this. A live preview shows your
character using your weapon on target dummies, so you can see how it feels
before you commit. There is also a "Hangout" room where everyone's characters
walk around, and an optional PvP mode there for fun.


4. WHAT YOU DO IN A FIGHT
-------------------------

Controls, by default:
- Move: WASD or arrow keys. Aim: mouse.
- Shoot: hold left click. Weapon behaviour depends on what you picked.
- Space: your role ability. F: your ultimate.
- Right click: parry. Hold it to raise a small shield on the side facing your
  cursor. It blocks bullets and shoves melee enemies back. If you raise it
  just as a shot arrives, that is a "perfect parry" and the bullet is sent
  back at the enemies. You cannot shoot while parrying and you move slower.
- Q, E and Shift: extra abilities you earn during the run.
- Tab: shows all your stats and upgrades.
- Pings (middle mouse) and a chat line let teammates talk without voice.
Every key can be changed in the settings.

Your abilities and ultimate:
- Space abilities have cooldowns. Examples: a Tank's Barrier blocks bullets,
  a Healer's Mend Pulse heals everyone nearby, a DPS Soldier's Dash gets you
  out of trouble.
- The ultimate (F) is a big move with a long cooldown, around a minute or more.
  You start each match half charged. Examples: Fortress puts up a dome that
  keeps enemies out; Lifeline revives and fully heals the whole party; Bullet
  Rain drops a damaging storm on the spot you aim at.

When you go down (0 health), you are "downed", not dead. A teammate stands next
to you for about 4 seconds to revive you, or a Healer can pulse you back.
When a wave is cleared, anyone who is down gets back up and everyone heals a
bit.

Every enemy killed drops XP for everyone. Some kills drop small green health
orbs (heal 15) or, rarely, big gold-ringed ones (heal 60). Bosses always drop
orbs.


5. THE GAMEPLAY LOOP
--------------------

A run repeats the same cycle:

1. The wave starts. Enemies pour in, many in small squads that come from one
   edge together. You dodge, shoot, use abilities, and help your team.
2. The wave is cleared. A "Wave cleared" box shows the clear time, score, and
   per-player kills, damage dealt and damage taken, with small awards like
   "Untouched" and "Reviver".
3. You level up. Everyone who gained levels this wave picks upgrades from cards
   (see section 6).
4. Sometimes, a bonus event happens during the break: a portal to a challenge
   room, a treasure chest, or a shop.
5. Everyone presses Ready. The next wave only starts once all players are
   ready, so nobody is rushed. There is an "auto-ready" option for people who
   want the pace to be quicker.
6. Every 5th wave, instead of normal enemies, a boss arrives. Beating it gives
   special rewards, and then the host picks a "path" for the next stretch.

Then it all repeats, getting harder each time, until wave 50.


6. LEVELS AND UPGRADES
----------------------

Upgrades are what make each run different. After a wave, you get three cards
for each level you gained. You pick one. You can "reroll" the cards if you do
not like them (you start with 2 rerolls and gain 2 more per boss killed).

Cards come in rarities, from plain to special:
- Common (white): small boosts, like more damage, more fire rate, more health,
  faster movement.
- Uncommon (green): bigger boosts and damage reduction.
- Rare (blue): upgrades for specific abilities, a "Second Charge" for your
  Space ability, extra projectiles, piercing shots.
- Very rare (purple): a brand-new ability on Q, E or Shift. You choose which
  key. These can be universal abilities (Shockwave, Blink, Nova, Turret,
  Overdrive) or your class's other abilities that you did not pick at the start.
- Legendary (orange): unique weapon upgrades that only come from bosses. Each
  boss offers its own set of three. For example, the Warden can give you a
  guard who fights at your side.
- Weapon mods (teal): after every boss you can also pick any "mod" that changes
  how your shots behave, like homing, ricochet, burning, freezing, shock,
  explosive or faster rounds. Each can stack up to three times.
- Milestone (gold): every 10 levels you get an extra pick from a special pool,
  such as more XP, more cards per level-up, more rerolls, or extra health.

Abilities also get stronger the more you use them. Each ability has a mastery
level from 1 to 5. Each level adds a bit of power and shortens the cooldown, and
level 5 unlocks a "capstone", which changes how the ability works (a Barrier
that reflects bullets, a Nova that fires twice as many shots, and so on).
Mastery resets every run.

Parry has its own upgrade cards too, such as a sturdier shield or a longer
window for perfect parries.


7. ENEMIES
----------

The early waves are mostly melee "rushers" that run at you and hurt on contact.
Shooters take over more and more as waves go up. From wave 13 on, the early
rushers are mostly gone.

New enemy types appear as you go. A few examples:
- Grunts fire small aimed spreads. Bursters fire slow rings of bullets that you
  can slip through. Snipers fire fast, accurate shots from far away.
- Chargers flash a warning line and then dash at you.
- Mortars lob bombs at where you are standing.
- Shielders have a front shield that blocks your shots, so you have to flank.
- Medics heal other enemies, so you want to kill them first.
- Phantoms blink next to you. Beam Sentries plant themselves and sweep a long
  beam across the screen. Leaders aim where you will be, not where you are.
- Juggernauts and Reavers are armoured and cannot be slowed or pushed.
- Nests are stationary and keep hatching small enemies until destroyed.

From wave 12, any enemy can show up as an "elite" (a gold ring): tougher, worth
more XP, always dropping an orb, and carrying special traits (faster, shielded,
explodes when killed, splits in two, reflects some of your shots, or buffs
nearby allies). Later waves can give an elite two traits.

The game is designed so that you can read what is coming: enemies glow just
before they fire, bullet shapes tell you what they will do (darts are aimed,
crescents curve, rounds are rings), and the number of bullets on screen is
capped, with only a few shooters firing at a time. Later waves get harder
mostly because enemies get tougher and hit harder, not because the screen fills
with more bullets. Enemy damage grows a few percent every wave.

Status effects are shown by icons over enemies: frozen, slowed, burning,
bleeding and marked (takes extra damage).


8. BOSSES
---------

There are ten bosses, one every 5 waves. Each one is a long, multi-stage fight
with its own mechanics, and each stage shows a clear warning before its attacks:

- Wave 5, The Warden: a prison riot. Searchlights, gates of push-back bullets,
  tear gas, and rioters pouring out of cells.
- Wave 10, Hive Queen: drones, bombers and dives.
- Wave 15, Twin Reapers: two bosses at once, tied together by a soul bond.
- Wave 20, The Singularity: a black hole with gravity wells that bend your shots.
- Wave 25, The Colossus: a stone giant that breaks down as it goes.
- Wave 30, The Duelist: a one-on-one duel of lunges and guards.
- Wave 35, The Lich: graves, soul beams and the dead rising.
- Wave 40, Storm Herald: lightning strikes and storms.
- Wave 45, The Devourer: grows bigger with each stage.
- Wave 50, The Eclipse: the final boss, with five stages and a last stand. It is
  about twice as long as any other boss.

Boss health is set so a fight lasts roughly one to two minutes. It also scales
with your party size and with how strong your build is, so a party with a very
strong build meets a tougher boss. Bosses never take turns shooting like normal
enemies do. They always fire.

The four waves before a boss are its "approach". Part of each wave is made of
that boss's own kind of enemy, so you meet its world on the way. Right now the
Warden and the Lich have their own faction of enemies for this.

When a boss changes stage, a few health orbs are knocked loose to help you.
After the boss dies, each player picks a legendary upgrade and a weapon mod.


9. PATHS, CHALLENGE ROOMS AND SHOPS
-----------------------------------

PATHS. After each boss, the host chooses one of two "paths" for the next four
regular waves, or no path. A path changes the enemy mix and the floor, and it
pays a prize before the next boss based on how risky it is (one to three
skulls). Examples:
- Swarm Fields: lots of melee enemies, but weaker.
- Sniper Ridge: mostly long-range shooters.
- Elite Gauntlet: half as many enemies, but all elite.
- Burning Grounds: patches of fire spread across the floor.
- Sanctuary: healing pools and fewer enemies (the easy one).
- Frozen Lake: you slide as if on ice.
- Blood Moon: no health drops and no regen, but kills heal you.
- Fogbound: you can only see close to your team.
- Narrow Pass: walls close in and wrapping is turned off.
- Bazaar: every kill drops gold, which you spend in a shop (see below).
Riskier paths give bonus XP and score while you are on them, plus better
prizes: an extra upgrade pick or even an extra legendary pick.

CHALLENGE ROOMS. Once in every five waves, a portal opens during the break.
If everyone steps in, you go to a special room with one of five challenges:
defend a crystal for 40 seconds, beat a mini-boss, kill 40 enemies in 40
seconds, hold three zones in turn, or beat shadow copies of yourselves. Win and
you get a prize pick of rare or very rare cards (sometimes a legendary too).
Lose and you are simply sent back with nothing lost, so it is safe to fail.
If you ignore the portal it closes on its own.

THE BAZAAR SHOP. On the Bazaar path, kills drop gold for everyone. Before the
boss, each player gets a shop with a shelf of upgrades picked for their class,
plus services like restocking, removing an old upgrade, a full heal, or a
"revive token" that gets you back up automatically once. You can also give gold
to teammates. Leftover gold turns into XP when the shop closes.

TREASURE CHESTS. Rarely, a chest appears. When opened, each player gets their
own private item card that only they can see. They can take it or throw it away,
and the wave waits until everyone has decided.


10. DIFFICULTY AND MODIFIERS
----------------------------

The host picks a difficulty before the run: Easy, Normal, Hard, Nightmare, or
Custom. These change things like enemy health, damage, speed, how many enemies
there are, how fast you regenerate, how long revives take, and more. In Custom,
the host can set each number. Party size also matters: solo players face weaker
enemies, and three-player parties face more and tougher ones.

On top of that, the host can turn on "modifiers", 17 switches that change the
whole run. They are grouped as Easier, Risky and Harder. Some examples:
- Easier: Lucky (extra rerolls), Head Start (start at level 3), Second Wind (a
  free self-revive once per boss cycle), Gold Rush (shops before every boss).
- Risky: Glass Cannon (everyone deals and takes 50% more damage), Hunted (one
  player at a time draws all the enemies but deals double damage), Bloodlust.
- Harder: Tough Crowd (more enemy health), Frenzy (faster fire), Swift Foes,
  Famine (no health drops), Tiny Arena (walls close in after each boss),
  Nightfall (the arena goes dark), Death Throes (enemies burst into bullet
  rings when killed), Solid Walls, Cluttered (lots of crates), and Zombie
  Infection (every regular wave becomes a horde of zombies of seven kinds).
Modifiers raise the XP you earn for your account rank.


11. OTHER MODES
---------------

- Run: the main 50-wave game.
- Boss Hunt: a short run of ten waves ending in one boss, using only that boss's
  own enemies. You unlock a boss's hunt by beating that boss in a normal run.
  Right now only the Warden has a hunt.
- Practice Range: you build your own waves from any enemies and bosses, set how
  strong they are, and practise against them. Setups can be saved and shared. It
  does not count towards your records or rank.
- Boss Lab and a wave editor are in the developer tools, for testing.


12. PERKS
---------

Winning a Boss Hunt unlocks a "perk" for that boss. You equip perks in the lobby.
You start with one slot, get a second after winning a full run, and a third
after winning five different hunts. The two perks that exist so far belong to
the Warden:
- Riot Gear: a shield plate blocks the next hit that would hurt you, then
  takes 30 seconds to come back.
- Contraband: a treasure chest always appears on the first wave after each boss,
  and your own item from any chest is rare or better.


13. PROGRESSION BETWEEN RUNS
----------------------------

Runs save themselves at the start of each wave, so you can quit and continue
later with everyone's characters, upgrades and progress restored. A lost or
won run loses its save.

Your profile (up to three per computer) tracks records: runs won and lost,
best wave, best score, time played, kills, bosses beaten, and wins per class.

You also have an account Rank. Each finished run earns XP (20 per wave cleared,
150 per boss, 40 per revive, 500 for a win, multiplied by modifiers and
difficulty). Rank is purely cosmetic: it never makes you stronger. It unlocks
things like titles, trails behind your character, frames for your name card,
menu backgrounds, name effects, aim-line styles, cursors and emotes. The tiers
go Recruit, Challenger, Veteran, Champion and Legend.

At the end of a run you get a results screen with the wave reached, time, score,
kills, and a card per player with damage, kills, healing, revives and awards
such as "Most damage" or "Best healer".


14. PLAYING WITH FRIENDS
------------------------

One player hosts and the others join over the network (the game suggests a
free VPN tool called Tailscale, or you can forward TCP port 7777 on a router).
Everyone needs the same game version. Players pick their loadouts, press Ready,
and the host starts the match. Teammates see each other's pings, emotes and
chat. Everyone's records and rank grow from the same run.


15. WHAT MAKES THE GAME WORK
----------------------------

In short, the game rests on a few ideas:
- Readable danger: warnings, bullet shapes and caps so that a hit feels fair.
- Teamwork: roles that matter (tank draws fire, healer keeps people up, DPS
  kills), revives, shared XP, and abilities that help allies.
- Build variety: six classes, three roles each, many weapons and abilities, then
  hundreds of upgrade combinations, so no two runs feel the same.
- Escalation: more enemy types, elites, paths, and ten very different bosses
  across 50 waves.
- Replayability: difficulties, modifiers, paths, boss hunts, perks and cosmetic
  ranks give reasons to run it again.

Under the hood, the game's rules are plain C# code kept separate from Unity
(in Assets/Scripts/Core), with the Unity-specific drawing and input in
Assets/Scripts/Game. The Core code runs on the host and is checked by a set of
simulation tests that run without Unity.
