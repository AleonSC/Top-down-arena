Who's playing, on floppy disks

The profiles screen with each profile as a 3.5-inch disk, and Manage profiles drawn as a keycap like the main menu's Play button (the game's KeyUi.Key: a dark well, a bevelled blue cap with a dished top, an engraved legend and a lamp that lights green under the mouse). Three layouts for the same disks.

Try it: hover a disk, click one to play as it, press Manage profiles for Rename and Delete (Delete asks twice, and an emptied slot lets you add a new profile).

Option AShelf

Four disks in a row where the four cards are now. Closest to today's layout, so nothing about how you scan or click changes.

  • The disk is the same size as the old card. Rank badge, name, title and rank, wins and runs all sit on the paper label, with the name in felt-tip.
  • The plastic is tinted by rank tier, as the card glow is today (gold for Legend, steel for Recruit). Your last-used profile gets a red stamp on its label.
  • New profile is a dashed outline of a disk. With all three slots used it dims and says so.

Option BScattered

Disks dropped on a desk at slightly different angles, overlapping a little. The loosest and most playful of the three.

  • Each disk straightens and lifts on hover, and comes to the front so the label is never covered.
  • The tilt is fixed per slot, so the screen looks the same every launch.
  • In Manage the disks straighten so the Rename and Delete buttons line up under them.

Option CDisk tray

The disks stand in a storage tray built like the main menu's panel, with corner brackets. Choosing a profile pulls its disk up out of the tray.

  • The tray ties the screen to the main menu box. The front lip hides the bottom of each disk and carries the slot numbers.
  • In Manage the slot numbers swap for Rename and Delete on the lip.
  • Hovering lifts a disk out of the tray far enough to read its wins and runs. At rest you see the name and rank.
