Halberd: a weapon you swing

Today the Knight's Halberd is a 52 px stick on the model, and when you attack the whole pole is drawn as a flat effect that appears over the hit, with nobody holding it. These looks keep every number (30 damage, 200 px reach, an 18° blade swept across 80° in 0.21 s, once per enemy per sweep, the return sweep at 60%) and change what you see: the Knight holds the pole in two gloved hands, winds it back, drives it through the arc with the pole sliding forward in his grip, turns the head over for the return cut, follows through and brings it back to the ready.

Pick a look, switch the return sweep on and off, slow it down, or drag the scrubber to stop on any frame.

    What moves

    • Both hands stay on the pole. The rear hand pushes, the front hand guides, and each is joined to a shoulder by an arm in your colour. The shoulders turn with the swing, about a third of the pole's angle.
    • The pole slides in the grip. Pulled back to a short carry while winding up, then it runs forward through the hands until the blade is at the full 200 px.
    • The blade is above the floor. It rises and grows as it is raised, drops as it is driven through, and a shadow on the ground shows the height.
    • The head turns over for the return cut. The axe edge always leads. In the gap between the two sweeps the head rolls half a turn, so for a moment it is seen edge-on.
    • The hit lands when the edge gets there. Each enemy flashes, recoils, takes a cut mark and shows its damage as the blade crosses it. 30, then 18 on the return; a grunt with 40 health goes down on the second.

    Kept as it is

    • Damage 30, reach 200 px, 18° blade, 80° sweep, six steps 0.035 s apart (0.21 s), each enemy hit once per sweep.
    • Return sweep with the extra-projectile upgrade: 60% damage, 0.08 s after the first sweep.
    • No change to the network: this is client-side drawing only, so Session.ProtocolVersion stays.

    Needs your call

    • Wind-up. "Off" keeps hits landing the moment you click: the pole rests already drawn back and only snaps through. "0.12 s" adds a visible draw-back and holds the hit 0.12 s after the click, which is the more satisfying read and the heavier feel, but a real change to the weapon's responsiveness.
    • The hit fan (grey, dashed) is only in the mockup to show the 200 px / 80° area. It would not be drawn in the game.
    • If one look is picked, the other two are dropped.
