Repulsion Cannon visuals

The Soldier Healer's Repulsion Cannon blasts a 70° cone out to 240 px: 6 damage, a 90 px shove (bosses and heavies don't budge), and enemies it hits sometimes shed a tiny health orb. Today that's a pale flash of the cone, three thin pressure arcs and a few streaks. Below are four other looks, drawn at the game's real cone, reach and timing, with today's look for comparison. Hits, shove and timing don't change: this is drawing only.

Notes for building it

  • All of this is drawing, on each screen. The blast is FxKind.Repulse, drawn by DrawRepulseFx in Game/WorldEffects.cs; the cone, shove (Knock.cs) and orbs stay in Core/Repulsion.cs, so none of these need a protocol change.
  • The hit feedback (a white flash on each enemy hit, a streak and dust along its slide, a small green orb popping out) is partly there already: the slide has its own streak, dust and impact puff (Knock.cs draws it). The toggle shows each look with and without the extra pieces, so you can tell what is the look and what is the feedback.
  • The cone is wider with extra projectiles (+20° each) and narrower with Homing Rounds; every look is built from the real half angle and reach, shown here at the base 70° and 240 px.
  • Look C bends the floor grid. That is a small change to the floor drawing for the blast's 0.5 s, only with Visual effects on, and only the grid lines (no gameplay). It is the one look that touches more than the effect itself.
  • Look D shakes the screen a little on firing. It would use the existing shake in Juice.cs (so Screen shake and Reduced effects already turn it down), and a weapon this fast would need it small.
  • The cannon fires fast. Whatever the look, it has to stay readable stacked: with three Healers firing, the cone should stay a clear shape and not wash the screen. The looks here are drawn at a firing interval you can compare by pressing Fire again quickly.
  • With Visual effects off, the arena should draw exactly as now (the render check stays IDENTICAL). The new look only shows with effects on.
