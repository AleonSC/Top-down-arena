Light Cycle Trail

A light cycle leaves a solid wall of light behind it, and it can only turn at right angles. This trail does both: it follows where you have been but snaps the path to long straight runs with sharp corners, so a swerve becomes an L. The wall lasts about two seconds and fades from the tail. Below are four ways to draw it, in three colours. The faint grid is the 40 px grid the wall snaps to, and your character still moves freely: only the trail is square.

Where it fits

It is the most dramatic trail, so I'd put it past Prism as the top trail reward, with the other two colours as free variants once you have it.

Build notes

  • Drawing only. Like every rank trail it is the client replaying where a player has been (Game/RankTrails.cs), so every screen draws everyone's trail from the lobby's trail number and nothing new goes over the network.
  • The snapping. Instead of a point every 7 px, this trail keeps one point per corner. It holds the wall on one axis until you drift 50 px sideways, then turns 90 degrees there. That is a handful of points (about 6 to 12) rather than 30, so it is cheaper than Ember. The last stretch from the last corner to you is drawn as a short faint link, so the wall always reaches your character.
  • Lasts longer. The wall lives about 2.4 s instead of 0.9 s, so it is a thin line of walls behind a moving player. Everything is kept at low alpha, under the player and under all projectiles, so a bullet crossing the wall stays easy to see. With Visual effects off it is one flat line, no glow.
  • Colour. Cyan is the classic. Orange and magenta would be equip options on the same trail (a small colour row in the cosmetics window). The id would stay one cosmetic, with the colour stored next to it in the profile.
  • Protocol. One new entry at the end of Rank.Cosmetics, so no existing number moves. The colour choice adds one byte to the cosmetics message and the lobby entry, so the protocol would be bumped.
  • My pick: B, Derezz. The wall reads as light while it is fresh, then breaks into squares that drift off and fade, which is the signature look from the films and also thins the trail out when it is oldest. A is the plain version if you want the quietest one.
