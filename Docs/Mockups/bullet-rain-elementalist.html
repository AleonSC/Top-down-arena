Bullet Rain

Real rain. The Elementalist raises the staff and calls a storm, then hones the raindrops into needles: each drop falls soft and blue, flashes partway down as it hardens, and lands as a sharp round. Four ways to show that. Every one has the same moment of change, so you can watch a drop turn from water into a weapon. Nothing here is in the game yet, and the damage and rate numbers are placeholders.

LOOK A

Honed Rain

What you see

A beam from the staff calls the storm. Thin blue drops fall, glint halfway down and become long glassy needles. Each lands with a ring and a splash, and leaves a puddle ripple.

Magic feel

The plainest and most literal version: it is clearly water. Cheapest to draw and the easiest to read for allies.

LOOK B

Frost Needles

What you see

Same storm, but the drops freeze mid-fall into pointed ice spears with a diamond tip and a glitter trail. Each shatters into shards and leaves a small frost patch.

Magic feel

Strong silhouette, and a natural fit for the class. A frost patch could chill or slow, if you want the rain to do more than damage.

LOOK C

Steel Rain

What you see

The drops turn metallic: a bright cross-flash as they harden, then thin silver needles. They land with a pale ring and a few orange sparks, like steel on stone.

Magic feel

The most weapon-like look, and the one that best sells that the water became bullets. Least magical of the four, more industrial.

LOOK D

Downpour

What you see

The whole arena darkens and rains, with ripples everywhere and an occasional lightning flash. Only the drops over your zone harden into needles. Everything outside it falls soft and harmless.

Magic feel

The most atmospheric. It changes the whole screen for six seconds, so it has the biggest effect cost and the most need for a Reduced effects fallback.
